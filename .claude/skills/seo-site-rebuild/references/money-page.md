# The Money Page

The single most important SEO task in the rebuild (Steps 9–11). Ship it before
the homepage redesign, before the blog, before anything.

## 1. Competitor teardown — do this first

Search the primary keyword. Open the top ~5 organic results that are genuinely
competing (skip directories and aggregators unless the whole page one is
directories — that's a signal you're targeting the wrong phrase, see Step 6).

Record for each, in a table:

| Competitor | H1 | H2s | Offer | Pricing shown? | Testimonials | FAQs | Portfolio | Primary CTA | ~Words | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Then answer explicitly: **what is the shared weakness across all five?** That
weakness is the rebuild's angle. Common ones in UK small-business web design:
no pricing, stock photography instead of real work, vague timelines, no named
person, agency tone aimed at brands with budgets the reader doesn't have.

## 2. Structure

```
H1   Small Business Website Design in <City>
     Opening: who it's for + the problem + primary CTA (above the fold)
H2   Websites Designed to Generate Enquiries      ← outcome, not features
H2   What's Included                              ← scannable list
H2   Who We Work With                             ← the narrow customer profile
H2   Our Website Design Process                   ← numbered, with timescales
H2   Pricing / Packages                           ← real numbers or real ranges
H2   Examples                                     ← portfolio or labelled demos
H2   Why Work With <Business>                     ← the differentiator
H2   FAQs                                         ← real questions, FAQPage schema
H2   Book a Consultation / Request a Quote        ← the close
```

Rules:
- The CTA appears above the fold, mid-page, and at the end. Same offer, same words.
- Process gets **timescales**. "2 weeks from brief to launch" outperforms
  "a collaborative discovery phase".
- Pricing gets **numbers**. "From £X" with what changes the price beats "get in
  touch for a quote", and it filters out the enquiries you don't want.
- Length is whatever covers the topic completely. Match the top results, then add
  the section they all skipped. Don't pad to hit a word count.
- It is a sales page that happens to rank, not an SEO essay.

## 3. Differentiators competitors cannot copy (Step 11)

Prioritise these in build order — cheapest proof first:

1. Transparent pricing and a published timeline (free, immediate, rare)
2. The exact process, step by step, with what the client has to do
3. Original FAQs taken from real customer conversations
4. Screenshots and before/after designs
5. Portfolio — real projects, or **clearly labelled** demonstrator sites
6. Client results with real numbers, once they exist
7. Video walkthrough of a build

Never fabricate items 5–7. A page with honest demonstrators and transparent
pricing outranks and outconverts a page with invented testimonials, and it
doesn't end in a trading-standards problem.

## 4. Conversion elements

- Phone number and email visible, not hidden behind a form
- Form: name, business, what you need, budget band — four fields, not twelve
- Local proof: city name in H1, service area stated, NAP matching the Google
  Business Profile exactly
- Trust: real photo of the person, company number, registered address
- Speed: this page must be the fastest on the site
